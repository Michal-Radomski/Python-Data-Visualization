# What is Corner Foerstner

**Corner Förstner** (often spelled _Foerstner_) refers to the **Förstner / Foerstner corner detector**, a method in computer
vision that finds and precisely localizes **corner points** in an image by analyzing the structure of local image gradients.
It is closely related to other gradient‑based corner detectors (like Harris) but is designed to give both **good
localization** and **robustness to noise**.[^1][^3][^5][^7][^9]

---

### What the Förstner corner detector does

The Förstner operator looks at small windows in the image and computes an **auto‑correlation (or structure) matrix** $A$
built from first‑order derivatives $I_x$ and $I_y$:

$$
A =
\begin{bmatrix}
A_{xx} & A_{xy} \\
A_{xy} & A_{yy}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

where $A_{xx}, A_{xy}, A_{yy}$ are sums of squares and products of gradients, usually averaged with a Gaussian
kernel.[^5][^1]

Using this matrix, the detector:

- Estimates how “corner‑like” a region is (by analyzing the eigenvalues of $A$).[^9][^1]
- Computes **error ellipses** that describe the **localization accuracy** and **roundness** of the detected corner.[^1][^5]

---

### Typical outputs: `w` and `q`

In implementations such as `skimage.feature.corner_foerstner`, the detector returns two arrays:

- `w`: measures **localization accuracy** (small ellipses mean well‑localized corners).[^5][^1]
- `q`: measures **roundness** (how close the corner’s error ellipse is to a circle, indicating “clean” corners).[^1][^5]

Thresholding on `w` and `q` then gives candidate corner points, and these can be further refined with non‑maxima suppression
to keep only the best, distinct corners.[^7][^5]

---

### How it differs from Harris

Both Förstner and Harris use the gradient auto‑correlation matrix, but:

- **Harris** typically uses a scalar response function $\det(A) - \kappa\,\text{trace}(A)^2$ to score corners.[^3][^9]
- **Förstner** focuses more explicitly on the **error ellipse** (size and shape) of the estimated corner position, which
  makes it attractive when you care about sub‑pixel localization accuracy.[^7][^9][^1]

In practice, the Förstner corner detector is used whenever you want **precise, well‑localized corner points**, for example in
image‑matching, stereo‑vision, or structure‑from‑motion pipelines.[^3][^7]
