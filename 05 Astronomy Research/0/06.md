**Meijering** and **Sato** filters are both **ridge‑detection (or “tubeness”) filters** used in image processing to enhance
long, thin, tube‑like structures such as blood vessels, neurites, wrinkles, or rivers in 2D or 3D images. They are commonly
available in libraries like `scikit‑image` and are especially popular in medical and biological imaging.
[tutorialspoint](https://www.tutorialspoint.com/scikit-image/scikit-image-meijering-neuriteness-filter.htm)

---

### Meijering (neuriteness) filter

The **Meijering neuriteness filter** enhances **ridge‑like structures** by analyzing the **eigenvalues of the Hessian
matrix** of the image intensity at multiple scales. It is designed to highlight long, thin objects such as **neurites** in
fluorescence microscopy, but it can also be used for wrinkles, rivers, or any continuous ridge‑like pattern.
[jait](https://www.jait.us/uploadfile/2024/JAIT-V15N1-132.pdf)

Key points:

- Works on 2D and 3D images.
  [pydocs.github](https://pydocs.github.io/p/skimage/0.17.2/api/skimage.filters.ridges.meijering.html)
- Uses a parameter `sigmas` to apply the filter at several smoothing scales, so it can respond to structures of different
  widths. [tutorialspoint](https://www.tutorialspoint.com/scikit-image/scikit-image-meijering-neuriteness-filter.htm)
- The user can choose whether ridges appear as **black** or **white** (via the `black_ridges` flag).
  [pydocs.github](https://pydocs.github.io/p/skimage/0.17.2/api/skimage.filters.ridges.meijering.html)

---

### Sato (tubeness) filter

The **Sato tubeness filter** is another **ridge‑enhancement filter** that also operates on the eigenvalues of the Hessian
matrix but is often considered a **faster**, simpler alternative to similar methods like the Frangi filter. It is designed to
enhance **vessel‑like structures**, such as blood vessels or tubular objects, in 2D and 3D images.
[tutorialspoint](https://www.tutorialspoint.com/scikit-image/scikit-image-sato-filter.htm)

Key points:

- Acts as a **“vesselness”** or **“tubeness”** measure: bright output values indicate regions that look like thin, elongated
  tubes. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10646760/)
- Also uses a multi‑scale approach (`sigmas`) to capture vessels of different diameters.
  [scikit-image](https://scikit-image.org/docs/stable/api/skimage.filters.html)
- Like Meijering, it supports **black ridges** vs **white ridges**, depending on whether the tubular structures are darker or
  brighter than the background. [tutorialspoint](https://www.tutorialspoint.com/scikit-image/scikit-image-sato-filter.htm)

---

### How they differ (roughly)

- Both are **Hessian‑based ridge filters**, but they use slightly different **response functions** on the eigenvalues to
  define “ridge‑ness” vs “tubeness.” [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10646760/)
- **Meijering** is tuned toward **neurite‑like structures** and emphasizes **continuous ridges**; **Sato** is tuned toward
  **3D tubular structures**, especially in medical imaging.
  [tutorialspoint](https://www.tutorialspoint.com/scikit-image/scikit-image-meijering-neuriteness-filter.htm)

In practice, people often try both and choose whichever gives cleaner enhancement for their specific type of tubular or
ridge‑like pattern. [scikit-image](https://scikit-image.org/docs/0.25.x/auto_examples/edges/plot_ridge_filter.html)
